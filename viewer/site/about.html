About this map

Where every regiment, battery and company of the American Civil War was, day by day, from 1861 to 1865, drawn from the war's own records.

This is a work in progress. Most positions are extracted from 19th-century texts by software, then checked against each unit's route and against other sources. Mistakes remain: a regiment placed at the wrong town of the same name, a march dated by its start rather than its end, a heading that names a corps one of whose regiments was elsewhere. Every position on the map shows its source; please report what you find.

Reading the map

A solid dot is a unit a source places there that day. A faint dot is an estimate: between two known places, its last known place, a long posting (“duty at Washington till March”), or where its brigade or campaign was that day. Click a unit for its positions through the year, each with its source, and use “Report a problem” there to send a correction with the unit and date already filled in. The legend on the map explains trails, brigade circles and the timeline.

Sources

  • Frederick H. Dyer, A Compendium of the War of the Rebellion (1908): the service record of every Union regiment, battery and company. Public domain; scans from the Internet Archive.
  • The War of the Rebellion: A Compilation of the Official Records of the Union and Confederate Armies (1880–1901): organization tables, station lists and the dated headings of reports and letters. Public domain; texts from the Internet Archive.
  • National Park Service: the Soldiers and Sailors Database (unit histories, including Crute’s Units of the Confederate States Army, and units engaged in battles) and the Civil War Sites Advisory Commission’s battle summaries, classes and campaigns, through Jeffrey B. Arnold’s acw_battle_data (BSD 3-Clause).
  • Wikipedia: orders of battle and regiment articles, CC BY-SA 4.0. Each placement taken from Wikipedia links to its article. Wikidata (CC0) for battles and their dates and places.
  • State unit histories: Clark’s Histories of the Several Regiments and Battalions from North Carolina (1901), Rowland’s Official and Statistical Register of Mississippi (1908), Lindsley’s Military Annals of Tennessee (1886) and Brewer’s Alabama (1872). Public domain.
  • Places: USGS Geographic Names Information System (public domain), with OpenStreetMap for the Petersburg and Washington forts and the Gettysburg monuments (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL).
  • Base map: Natural Earth (public domain); terrain shading from AWS Terrain Tiles (USGS 3DEP and SRTM elevation data).
  • Timeline events: battles from the NPS lists above; political, social and command events written for this map with links to Wikipedia.

How it is made

A pipeline reads each source into dated claims (“the 5th Iowa was at Chattanooga on November 20, 1863, per Dyer’s Compendium”), locates the places, links units across sources, and keeps each claim with its source. Where sources conflict or a place name is ambiguous, each unit’s own route decides: a placement that would need an impossible march is set aside. Between known positions, a unit is drawn moving along a straight line, or with its brigade or campaign. The code is open: github.com/petertuuk/civil_war_viewer.
