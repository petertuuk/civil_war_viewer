    Solid: a source places it there that day. Faint: an estimate: between two known places, its last known place, a long posting (“duty at Washington till March”), or where its brigade or its campaign was that day.
    Zoomed out, a larger circle is a brigade: three or more of its regiments within about 40 km of each other. Zoom in or turn off “Group brigades” to see them one by one.
    Trails: drag across the timeline below the slider, or pick a length, to see where units moved over those days; each line ends in an arrow where the unit was on the last day, and units on the same route share one, thicker line. Dashed: partly estimated. Dotted and faint: a step faster than a march (over 50 km a day), such as a move by rail or river, or a doubtful placement. Zoom the timeline with − and +, or the mouse wheel over it, to pick a few days or a whole campaign.
    Marks above the timeline: battles (the National Park Service's classes, the most decisive shown first), politics, society, command and strategy. Zoom the timeline in for more of them; click one to go to that day. The best-known events are named above their marks (lesser ones too once you zoom in to a year or so; names already passed are faint). The line under the date names what is under way or just happened. Below the marks: campaigns, shaded by theater (Eastern, Western, Trans-Mississippi, Lower Seaboard; from the National Park Service's battle list, dated by their first and last battles; click one to zoom to it), and the share of each side's placed units that moved 8 km or more since the day before (Union above the line, Confederate below; hover for the numbers). Drag the small bar at the top of the panel to make the timeline taller or shorter.
    Click a unit for where the sources place it through the year, each with its source.
    Zoomed in on a battlefield: regiments stand at their monuments, and a brigade's regiments are joined as its line.
    Positions from Dyer's Compendium, the Official Records, Wikipedia orders of battle, NPS lists and battlefield monuments; places located with USGS GNIS.
